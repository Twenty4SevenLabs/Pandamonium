# Play 1 — Extract the Buyer Problem

## Objective

Turn the owner's real experience into one narrow buyer/problem hypothesis worth researching.

## Required inputs

- Passed `AGENT-CAPABILITY-MAP.md`
- Owner's experience, repeatable methods, artifacts, and observed customer questions
- Any owned source material that may be curated

## Tool capability needed

- **Needed:** structured interviewing and document creation
- **Helpful:** file inventory/search
- **Manual fallback:** the owner lists past projects, recurring questions, and reusable artifacts in a document

## Ordered actions

1. Ask the owner to name three tasks or outcomes people repeatedly ask them to help with.
2. For each, capture who asks, the situation, current workaround, consequence, desired outcome, and evidence available.
3. Inventory owned source material. Record ownership, confidentiality, freshness, and whether it can be redistributed.
4. Exclude ideas that depend on private client data, unverifiable expertise, regulated advice, copied products, or results the owner cannot support.
5. Score the remaining ideas in your copied `offer-scorecard.csv`. For evidence strength, pain frequency, outcome clarity, owner advantage, and reachable audience, `1` means weak/low and `5` means strong/high. For build effort, support burden, and risk, `1` means low and `5` means high. Do not blindly add positive and negative dimensions; explain the tradeoff in `total_notes`.
6. Select one hypothesis. Treat the score as decision support, not proof of demand.
7. Write a one-sentence problem statement: “For [specific buyer] in [situation], [problem] causes [consequence], and current alternatives fall short because [gap].”
8. Define who is not the buyer and what outcome is explicitly out of scope.
9. Save `work/01-buyer/BUYER-PROBLEM-BRIEF.md` and update `STATUS.md`.

## Produced artifact

`BUYER-PROBLEM-BRIEF.md` with:

- named buyer and situation;
- problem, consequence, and current alternatives;
- owner advantage and owned-source inventory;
- evidence, assumptions, and unknowns;
- exclusions and risk boundaries;
- access hypothesis for reaching the buyer;
- selected idea and rejected alternatives.

## Success criteria

- The buyer is specific enough to recognize themselves.
- The problem is observable and not merely “wants more money.”
- At least two evidence types or owner observations are recorded; assumptions remain labeled.
- The owner has a legitimate knowledge or process advantage.
- Redistributable source ownership is known.
- The idea has a plausible zero-cost or approved-cost route to potential buyers.

## Stop conditions

- The only source is confidential client material or copied intellectual property.
- The outcome requires legal, medical, tax, investment, or security claims beyond the owner's supported competence.
- No specific buyer, consequence, or reachable context can be named.
